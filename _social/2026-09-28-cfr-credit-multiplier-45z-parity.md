# Social copy — Ottawa's Proposed Biofuel Credit Multiplier vs. US 45Z

Post: /_posts/2026-09-28-cfr-credit-multiplier-45z-parity.md

## LinkedIn

Ottawa is weighing a credit multiplier under the Clean Fuel Regulations so domestic biofuel earns more compliance credits than imports. ECCC says about 1.4 for biomass-based diesel and about 1.14 for ethanol would replicate what US producers get from the 45Z tax credit.

We rebuilt both numbers with 2026 rules ($1.09/gal 45Z amount after inflation indexing, ILUC removed from scoring).

Diesel: ECCC's 1.4 holds up. Parity for soy renewable diesel and biodiesel lands between about 1.37 and 1.46 at ECCC's CAD 300 credit price.

Ethanol: it depends on the benchmark, and we revised ours. Published Iowa plant scores (IRFA, 2023), converted to 45Z units and stripped of ILUC, put the average plant at about 51 kg CO2e/mmBtu, right at the 50 threshold, so roughly nothing from 45Z. But ECCC's own list of approved carbon intensities shows 28 US plants at 33 to 46 g CO2e/MJ, averaging about 40. Used as a proxy for 45Z scores, that is about US$0.18/gal for the typical plant and parity near 1.2 at CAD 300 (1.14 is reached only at about CAD 400). The best plants need 1.4. Carbon capture needs about 1.8. The proxy is uncertain by several points of score, and plant-level 45Z amounts are not public. Two public filings (Highwater, Green Plains) imply scores in the same range.

So one multiplier has to cover a US benefit that runs from about 3 cents to over 60 cents a gallon. And it's sensitive to the credit price: at CAD 200 the numbers climb sharply, at CAD 400 they fall.
