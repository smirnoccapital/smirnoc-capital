# Social copy — Washington Floated a Diesel Export Ban This Week

Post: /_posts/2026-09-24-diesel-export-ban-e15-refining-math.md

## LinkedIn

Diesel hit $6.53/gallon this week. Trump floated banning US diesel exports to fix it on Tuesday. By Wednesday the White House had backed off. We ran the refining math on why.

Refineries don't make diesel in isolation — one barrel of crude becomes gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel together, in close to fixed proportions. US refiners are already running at 97.8% utilization, the highest in years. There's no slack to make "more diesel" without cutting something else. That's exactly what Energy Secretary Chris Wright, API, and GasBuddy's Patrick De Haan said publicly this week: restrict exports, and refiners cut runs — taking gasoline and jet fuel down with diesel.

There's also a geography problem nobody in Washington seems to be tracking. The Gulf Coast supplies over 90% of US diesel exports (we did the math off EIA's own numbers: 1,127 kb/d of 1,250 kb/d in 2025). But the Midwest — Iowa included — gets its diesel from regional refineries in Minnesota, Illinois, Oklahoma, and Kansas, fed by pipelines that don't run through the Gulf. A Gulf Coast glut from a lost export market wouldn't flow north to the farmers a ban is supposed to help.

The more coherent lever is raising the ethanol blend from E10 to E15 — it doesn't need more crude runs at all, just a smaller petroleum share of the gasoline pool. It's real, and Washington's already using it (EPA's emergency E15 waiver, a year-round E15 bill in the Senate). But it has a ceiling nobody mentions: full nationwide E15 would need about 1.75 billion more gallons of ethanol a year than US plants currently have the capacity to make.
