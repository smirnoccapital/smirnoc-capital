# Social copy — Ottawa Says Farmers Now Face a Negative Tax Rate on New Equipment

Post: /_posts/2026-09-23-productivity-mega-deduction-farm-scenarios.md

## LinkedIn

Ottawa's new Productivity Mega Deduction gives 100% immediate expensing on new farm equipment starting September 15, 2026, and Finance Canada says it drops agriculture's marginal effective tax rate to -6.0%. We built three farm scenarios to see when that's actually true.

A farm buying steadily every year gets a real but modest benefit — it's a timing acceleration on deductions it would have gotten anyway, worth about $18,700 in present value over 8 years in our illustrative case.

A farm making one large, lumpy purchase — a new combine every 8-10 years, common in Ontario grain operations — is the interesting case. If the purchase exceeds that year's income, the excess becomes a non-capital loss. Left on its default carryforward, the Mega Deduction beats Budget 2025's existing Accelerated Investment Incentive by only about $11,000 in present value. Actively electing a 3-year loss carryback (not automatic — you have to ask for it) gets that up to roughly $70,000 — and only then does something like a genuine negative tax rate actually show up in cash.

Only a larger operation whose income comfortably absorbs the full purchase in the same year sees the clean version of the government's claim.

The takeaway isn't that the deduction is fake — it's that "100% immediate expensing" mostly changes *when* tax relief arrives, not *how much* a farm pays over an asset's life, and the size of that timing benefit depends heavily on how big your purchase is relative to your income in that specific year.
