# Social copy — Can the US Actually Get Meaningful Potash Out of Belarus?

Post: /_posts/2026-09-21-us-belarus-potash-feasibility.md

## LinkedIn

Trump says the US is working on a "massive" potash deal with Belarus, priced well below what Washington pays Canada. We checked the logistics.

Belarus has the reserves (21% of world reserves, 2nd to Canada) and is adding production capacity (Nezhinsky, +2mn t/yr, Q2 2026). But it lost its efficient export route in 2022 when Lithuania closed Klaipėda — a port that used to handle 9-11mn t/yr of Belarusian potash — under EU sanctions. Since then, everything moves through Russian rail and Russian-controlled Baltic ports that Belarus doesn't control and has to share with Russia's own (larger) potash exporter, Uralkali.

Getting that potash to the US would mean: rail through Russia -> Russian port -> transatlantic ocean freight -> US port -> inland transport to the Midwest. Canadian potash skips all of that — it's one direct unit-train ride from Saskatchewan straight into the US Corn Belt.

The historical evidence backs this up: even from 2017-2021, when Belarus had full access to the easy route, the US only imported ~635,000 t/yr from Belarus on average — about 5% of what it buys from Canada today. And most of what Belarus can currently export is already going to China (>70% of sales by 2023, up from 17% in 2021).

None of this means zero Belarusian potash reaches American farmers. It means "massive" is doing a lot of work in the current rhetoric that the logistics don't yet support. Full breakdown, with sources, on the site.
