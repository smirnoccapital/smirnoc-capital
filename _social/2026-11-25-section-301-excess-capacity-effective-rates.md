**Headline rates up to 35%. The real increase: under 8 points.**

On Nov. 25, USTR proposed new Section 301 "excess capacity" tariffs on 14 economies: 35% for Vietnam, 25% for India, 20% for China, 15–18% for most others. Headline rates don't tell you what importers will actually pay, so we estimated the effective rate for each economy.

How we did it:

1️⃣ **Start with what's paid today.** Every product line (HTS10) the US imported from each economy in Aug–Sep 2026, and the duties actually collected (US Census). Duties ÷ import value = today's effective rate.

2️⃣ **Remove what's exempt.** Section 232 goods (steel, aluminum, autos, wood, etc.; China is the exception) and ~2,100 annex product lines, including electronics, energy, pharma and many raw materials.

3️⃣ **Add only the gap.** A senior US official told Reuters the new duties won't stack on the 10–12.5% forced-labor tariff in place since July. On covered goods, we added the difference.

4️⃣ **Weight by trade.**

Worked example, Vietnam: today's effective rate is 7.0%. Only 29% of its US imports are covered. On that slice, the 35% rate adds 22.5 points (35 minus the 12.5 already paid). 29% × 22.5 ≈ +6.6 points, for a new effective rate of 13.6%.

Across all 14, that's roughly $76B a year in additional duties. Biggest movers: China 21.2% → 28.9%, Bangladesh 24.3% → 31.8%, Vietnam 7.0% → 13.6%, India 8.5% → 13.9%.

⚠️ **What could make these numbers wrong, and in which direction:**

🔺 **Too high if** importers shift toward exempt goods or other suppliers (we hold trade flows fixed), or final rates come in below the "up to" levels.

🔻 **Too low if:**
- USTR applies its fallback rates for "unsatisfactory" talks. China would reach ~51%.
- Trade returns to 2025's mix. Recent months are heavy in exempt AI hardware; on 2025 weights Vietnam's proposed rate is ~20%, not 13.6%.
- The final rules stack the new rates on the July tariff, contrary to officials' stated intent. Bangladesh would reach ~41%.

Rates are proposals only; comments are due Jan. 5. Full tables, including farm inputs, on the site.
