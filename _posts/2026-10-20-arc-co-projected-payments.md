**By Kal Sharven**

**TL;DR:** At USDA's projected 2026/27 market year average prices (corn $4.70, soybeans $12.00, wheat $6.30), and with 2025 county yields standing in for 2026, Agriculture Risk Coverage by county (ARC-CO) would pay about **$3.0 billion** on 2026 crops: **$1.90 billion on corn, $682 million on soybeans and $391 million on wheat**. Payments follow yield shortfalls against each county's five-year benchmark, so they do not track production. Iowa receives the most corn money ($284 million) at an average of about $24 a base acre, while Ohio and South Dakota average about $49.

## How ARC-CO pays

ARC-CO pays when a county's actual revenue (county yield times the national market year average price) falls below 90% of its benchmark revenue. The payment is the shortfall, capped at 12% of benchmark revenue, on 85% of a farm's enrolled ARC-CO base acres. Benchmarks for program year 2026 use FSA's 2020 to 2024 Olympic averages: $5.03 for corn, $12.17 for soybeans and $6.98 for wheat.

All three benchmark prices are above the projected prices, which keeps the guarantee high. At projected prices a county's yield has to fall below about 96% of its benchmark for corn, 91% for soybeans and 99.7% for wheat before a payment starts. Soybeans have the widest cushion, corn a narrow one, and wheat almost none.

**Corn.** Iowa ($284 million, 15% of the total), Illinois ($237 million, 12%) and South Dakota ($211 million, 11%) lead on dollars. Per base acre the order changes: South Dakota and Ohio average about $49, Indiana $30, Illinois $26 and Iowa $24. Large base acreage with modest shortfalls produces the big totals in Iowa and Illinois.

**Soybeans.** North Dakota is first at $140 million (21%), ahead of Illinois ($94 million), South Dakota ($78 million), Ohio ($73 million) and Kentucky ($53 million). Kentucky has the highest average rate of those states, at about $76 a base acre.

**Wheat.** Kansas ($95 million, 24%) and Washington ($63 million, 16%) lead. Counties with a yield estimate cover 71% of wheat base acres, so the total is a floor: scaled to all wheat base acres it is about $549 million.

## What the projection leaves out

- **Yields are a stand-in.** The model uses 2025 NASS county yields. Program year 2026 payments depend on 2026 county yields and on final market year average prices, which USDA publishes on June 30, 2027 for wheat and September 30, 2027 for corn and soybeans.
- **Totals are before sequestration and payment limits.** They include the 85% factor on enrolled base acres, using FSA's 2025 enrolled base acres as of January 14, 2026.
- **Split counties are averaged.** Where a county has both irrigated and non-irrigated rates, the total uses their simple average, which assumes equal acreage.
- **PLC is not mapped.** Price Loss Coverage pays on a separate price test and is calculated on farm payment yields, not county yields. At these prices the map's estimate is no PLC payment for corn or soybeans and about $0.05 a bushel for wheat.

## What it means

At these prices ARC-CO is a yield-shortfall program more than a price-support program for corn and soybeans: the average county does not trigger it, and the counties that do are the ones with the biggest gap to benchmark. Wheat is the exception, because its guarantee price (90% of $6.98, or $6.28) is almost exactly the projected price. For Ontario's farmers the useful comparison is how the guarantee is set: a rolling five-year average applied to county yields instead of the farm's own.

**What to watch**

- USDA's 2026 county yield estimates, which replace the 2025 stand-in
- Monthly changes to the projected 2026/27 prices, since a corn price below about $4.53 puts every county's guarantee above its revenue at benchmark yield
- FSA's published final program year 2026 benchmarks and rates
