**By Kal Sharven**

**TL;DR:** On November 25, the US Trade Representative proposed new Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies it says run "structural excess capacity" in manufacturing, with headline rates from 15% to 35%. Measured against everything the US actually imports from each economy, the increase is much smaller: **0.4 to 7.7 percentage points**. China's effective tariff rate rises from 21.2% to **28.9%**, Vietnam's from 7.0% to **13.6%**, India's from 8.5% to **13.9%** and the EU's from 6.5% to **8.1%**. The gap has two causes: most imports from these economies are exempt, and the new rates replace the 10% to 12.5% forced-labor tariffs in place since July instead of adding to them. Across the 14 economies the proposals add about **$76 billion a year** in US duties at current trade volumes.

## What USTR proposed

USTR made determinations in 14 of the 16 investigations it opened in March. It has not made determinations for Mexico or Norway. Each proposal comes with a lower rate tied to a trade agreement or, for most economies, a higher fallback rate if USTR finds consultations or existing commitments unsatisfactory.

| Economy | Proposed rate | Basis | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vietnam | up to 35% | net of existing Section 301 tariffs | 20% with a new trade agreement |
| India | up to 25% | net of existing Section 301 tariffs | 17% with a new trade agreement |
| Singapore | up to 20% | net of existing Section 301 tariffs | 10% with a new trade agreement |
| China | up to 20% (10% on Annex A goods) | net of the forced-labor Section 301 tariff | up to 40% fallback |
| Bangladesh | up to 18% | net of existing Section 301 tariffs | up to 20% fallback |
| Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand | up to 18% | net of existing Section 301 tariffs | up to 25% fallback |
| EU, Switzerland, Japan | 15% | net of MFN rates and existing Section 301 tariffs | up to 20% fallback |
| Korea | 15% | net of MFN rates and existing Section 301 tariffs | up to 25% fallback |
| Taiwan | 15% | net of MFN rates and existing Section 301 tariffs | up to 25% fallback, net of Section 301 tariffs only |

Written comments are due January 5, 2027, and hearings begin January 25, 2027. No effective date has been set.

## Why the headline rate overstates the increase

**The new rates absorb July's tariff.** Since July 24, 2026, imports from 60 economies have paid a Section 301 tariff over forced-labor enforcement: 12.5% for China, Singapore, Vietnam and Thailand; 10% for India, Malaysia, Indonesia, Cambodia and Bangladesh; and a cap of 10% (EU, Taiwan) or 12.5% (Japan, Korea, Switzerland) including the normal MFN duty. The new proposals are stated "net of" existing Section 301 tariffs, and a senior US official told Reuters the new duties "would not stack on top of tariffs of 10% or 12.5%" imposed in July. A Vietnamese good now paying 12.5% would pay 35% in total, an increase of 22.5 points. For the EU, Japan, Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan, 15% is an all-in ceiling including MFN, so the increase is at most 2.5 to 5 points.

China is the exception on stacking: per the same official, its 20% applies on top of the roughly 25% tariffs imposed during President Trump's first term.

**Most trade is exempt.** For 13 of the 14 economies the proposals exclude goods already subject to Section 232 tariffs (steel, aluminum, copper, passenger and commercial vehicles and parts, wood products, patented pharmaceuticals and semiconductors), along with an annex of about 2,100 tariff lines common to every notice: computers, semiconductors, smartphones, energy, many pharmaceutical and aircraft articles, and a long list of raw materials and products not grown or made in sufficient quantity in the US. Seven economies also get their own additional exemption lists. China's notice has no Section 232 exemption, applies 10% to the annex goods instead of exempting them, and exempts 77 products on the US-China Board of Trade's "30-for-30" list.

## Effective tariff rates, now and proposed

![Effective US tariff rate by economy, now and under the proposed Section 301 excess-capacity tariffs]({{ '/assets/images/301-excess-capacity-effective-rates.png' | relative_url }})

The effective rate is total duties divided by the value of all US imports from the economy, including duty-free and exempt goods, across every 10-digit tariff line (US Census Bureau imports for consumption, August and September 2026, the first full months under the July tariffs). The proposed rate adds, line by line, the gap between each economy's proposed rate and the forced-labor tariff it already pays, on the lines the proposals cover.

| Economy | Headline rate | Effective rate now | Effective rate proposed | Change (points) | Share of imports hit | With deal rate | With fallback rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| China | 20%\* | 21.2% | **28.9%** | +7.7 | 84% | – | 51.4% |
| Bangladesh | 18% | 24.3% | **31.8%** | +7.6 | 94% | – | 33.7% |
| Vietnam | 35% | 7.0% | **13.6%** | +6.6 | 29% | 9.2% | – |
| Cambodia | 18% | 20.2% | **26.5%** | +6.3 | 79% | – | 32.0% |
| India | 25% | 8.5% | **13.9%** | +5.3 | 35% | 11.0% | – |
| Indonesia | 18% | 14.1% | **19.1%** | +5.1 | 63% | – | 23.6% |
| EU | 15% all-in | 6.5% | **8.1%** | +1.6 | 34% | – | 9.8% |
| Malaysia | 18% | 3.6% | **5.1%** | +1.6 | 19% | – | 6.5% |
| Thailand | 18% | 5.2% | **6.4%** | +1.2 | 22% | – | 7.9% |
| Singapore | 20% | 1.8% | **2.7%** | +0.9 | 12% | 1.8% | – |
| Japan | 15% all-in | 10.5% | **11.3%** | +0.8 | 32% | – | 12.9% |
| Switzerland | 15% all-in | 3.6% | **4.1%** | +0.5 | 20% | – | 5.1% |
| Taiwan | 15% all-in | 2.6% | **3.0%** | +0.5 | 9% | – | 4.1% |
| Korea | 15% all-in | 6.6% | **7.0%** | +0.4 | 17% | – | 8.7% |

\*China: 20% net of the 12.5% forced-labor tariff, on top of first-term Section 301 tariffs; 10% on annex goods; no Section 232 exemption. Singapore's 10% deal rate is below its 12.5% forced-labor rate, so it leaves Singapore unchanged.

**Worked example, Vietnam.** Vietnam's effective rate today is 7.0%. Of its US imports, 29% by value is covered by the proposal; the rest is electronics, other annex goods and Section 232 products. On the covered 29%, the rate rises by 22.5 points (35% minus the 12.5% already paid). That adds 0.29 × 22.5 ≈ 6.6 points, for an effective rate of 13.6%.

**Where the bite is largest.** Bangladesh and Cambodia ship mostly apparel and footwear, which nothing exempts: 94% and 79% of their US imports would pay the new rate. Taiwan sits at the other end. Computers, servers and chips make up most of what it sells the US, and only 9% of its imports are covered. Japan and Korea change little because their largest exports, vehicles and parts, are Section 232 goods that keep their current 15% rate.

## Farm inputs

The proposals tax US imports, so they change costs for US farmers, not for Ontario's farmers buying the same products in Canada. A few input categories show where the US cost changes. Values are annualized from August and September 2026 imports.

| Input (HS code) | Source | US imports, $ million/yr | Effective rate now | Proposed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organo-phosphorus compounds incl. glyphosate (2931) | China | 688 | 19.0% | 26.7% |
| Insecticides, herbicides, fungicides (3808) | India | 138 | 14.5% | 29.0% |
| Insecticides, herbicides, fungicides (3808) | China | 146 | 38.5% | 46.6% |
| Insecticides, herbicides, fungicides (3808) | EU | 247 | 9.9% | 14.3% |
| Harvesting machinery (8433) | EU | 558 | 11.2% | 13.9% |
| Harvesting machinery (8433) | China | 198 | 30.6% | 38.1% |
| Tractors (8701) | EU | 1,189 | 14.1% | 14.4% |
| Tractors (8701) | Japan | 675 | 15.0% | 15.0% |
| Tractors (8701) | Thailand | 342 | 14.7% | 20.2% |
| Fertilizers (31) | EU | 397 | 0.3% | 0.3% |

Crop protection products carry the largest increases. Indian pesticides would double from 14.5% to 29.0%, since almost all of them are covered and India's rate rises by 15 points. Fertilizer is on the exempt annex. Tractors from the EU, Japan and Korea already pay a 15% Section 232 rate and are exempt from the new tariff; tractors from Thailand are not.

Canada is not one of the 14 economies. Canadian goods pay the July forced-labor tariff of 10% only when they do not enter duty-free under USMCA.

## What the estimates leave out

- **Trade does not adjust.** The rates are first-round estimates on August and September 2026 trade. Importers shifting to exempt goods or other suppliers would pull the realized rate down.
- **Rates are ceilings.** Most proposals say "up to." Final rates could be lower, or the fallback rates could apply instead (China would reach 51.4%).
- **Recent trade is heavy in exempt electronics.** August and September 2026 imports from Taiwan, Korea and Vietnam are weighted toward servers and memory. On the 2025 import mix, Vietnam's proposed rate is 19.9% and India's 15.3%.
- **"Net of" is not defined in the notices.** The estimates follow the administration's stated intent that the new rates do not stack on the forced-labor tariff. If the final notices stack them, Bangladesh would reach about 41% and Indonesia about 26%.
- **Antidumping and countervailing duties are excluded.**

## What to watch

- Comments due January 5, 2027, and hearings starting January 25, 2027
- Final notices, which will set the rates, the effective date and how "net of" is applied
- Determinations for Mexico and Norway, which USTR has not yet made
- New trade agreements with Vietnam, India and Singapore, which carry lower rates
